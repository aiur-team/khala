# The honest privacy picture for an agent that sends E2EE plaintext to a hosted LLM

**Method note, because it affects how you should read this:** this session's WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200) before research began, so everything below comes from **direct fetches of primary policy pages**, not search-ranked summaries or recollection. That made discovery harder but the citations stronger — every quote below was pulled from the live page. Gaps are flagged explicitly as gaps, not silently filled.

## The one-paragraph answer

If your agent holds E2EE plaintext and ships it to a hosted LLM, **the end-to-end guarantee is gone the moment the plaintext leaves your process, and nothing you can buy in 2026 fully restores it.** What you can buy is (a) a *contractual* promise not to persist it — Zero Data Retention — which in 2026 has acquired a hole big enough to matter: **the most capable models are now categorically excluded from ZDR on all three major platforms**; or (b) a *cryptographic/hardware* attestation from a small set of TEE-native vendors, which is real, client-verifiable, and limited to open-weight models — and whose hardware root was demonstrably broken in 2025–26 by sub-$1,000 attacks executed by exactly the adversary (the operator with physical access) the whole category exists to exclude; or (c) self-hosting open weights, which in 2026 is genuinely competitive for short-horizon tool use and still ~30% behind on long-horizon agentic work. FHE and MPC are not options — they are 3–4 orders of magnitude too slow.

# 1. Zero data retention and no-training terms, provider by provider (September 2026)

## The headline finding: frontier capability XOR zero retention

This is new since 2025 and it is the single most important thing in this report. **Three independent platforms now carve the most capable models out of ZDR as a condition of access.**

**Anthropic direct.** The ZDR article ([privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8956058](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8956058-i-have-a-zero-data-retention-agreement-with-anthropic-what-products-does-it-apply-to), last updated **June 9, 2026**) says ZDR covers "eligible Anthropic APIs, Anthropic products that use your Commercial organization API key (including Claude Code accessed via the API), and Claude Code for Enterprise plans" — but that Anthropic still retains "User Safety classifier results in order to enforce our Usage Policy," and that "for **Covered Models**, we require limited data retention and review as a part of our safety work."

The Covered Models article ([privacy.claude.com/en/articles/15425996](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/15425996-data-retention-practices-for-covered-models)) is blunt: "**Prompts submitted to, and outputs generated by, covered models are retained for 30 days**," covering "Mythos-class models and future models with similar capabilities," including Claude Fable 5 and 5.1. It **overrides pre-existing ZDR**: organizations with ZDR workspaces must enable retention to access these models. Anthropic's mitigations are meaningful but organizational, not cryptographic — "No Anthropic personnel can read your retained conversations" by default; human review "only through a controlled access path"; "a small set of approved reviewers"; every access in "a tamper-proof log."

**AWS Bedrock.** [docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html): "Amazon Bedrock uses a **zero operator access (ZOA)** data security model… **no operators of the service can access model input or output**. Also… a **zero data retention (ZDR)** data security model… **by default, Amazon Bedrock does not store model inputs or outputs**." Then the carve-out: "For **Anthropic Claude Fable 5 and Claude Fable 5.1, ALL traffic** will be retained for up to **30 days**… Classifier-flagged traffic will be subject to potential **human review performed by AWS**." For the OpenAI GPT-5.4/5.5/5.6/GPT-6 Astra family, classifier-flagged traffic is retained 30 days. Customers in the **Enterprise Frontier Safeguards** program get ZDR only "**through December 31, 2026**" — an explicitly expiring exemption.

Bedrock did ship a genuinely good control in 2026: a first-class retention API with modes `none < default < aws_review < provider_data_share`, enforceable org-wide via an SCP on the `bedrock:DataRetentionMode` condition key. **This is the only technically-enforced retention control in this entire report.** Two warnings from the docs ([data-retention.html](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-retention.html)): "**Setting `store=false` does not guarantee zero data retention**," and under `none`, invoking a model that requires retention makes Bedrock "**block the request and return an error**" — Claude Fable 5/5.1 are simply unavailable under ZDR.

**Google Cloud.** The new [Advanced AI Safety Addendum](https://cloud.google.com/terms/advanced-ai-safety-addendum) (last modified **July 30, 2026**) is the biggest ZDR-breaker on GCP: it "constitutes Customer's instruction to Google to: (A) **store outside Customer's Account** certain Customer Data, specifically prompts and Generated Output, and (B) retain such Customer Data for **up to thirty (30) days** and, during that period, review it using automated tools or, as necessary, **human review by authorized personnel on a need-to-know basis**." Scope: "Claude Mythos (all versions), Claude Fable (all versions)." The [data governance page](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/data-governance) (updated 2026-09-15) adds: "To use Claude Fable 5 on Google Cloud and Mythos 5, **as required by Anthropic, you must enable sharing this data with Anthropic** for abuse monitoring." **Acceptance is a per-project console click gated on `aiplatform.consents.update` — any project admin can bind your org to it.**

# 2. What ZDR does and does not protect against

**Be blunt: ZDR is a promise not to `write()`. It is not a promise not to `read()`.** Every provider terminates TLS at their edge, decrypts, and hands plaintext to inference hosts. Your prompt sits in host RAM, in KV caches, in HBM, and in whatever the serving stack buffers. The vendors say this themselves when read carefully:

- Bedrock's `none` mode is "No request or response data is **written to durable storage**." Durable storage — not memory.
- Google, most explicitly: the 24h Vertex cache "is stored **only in-memory** (not at-rest)… **does not violate zero data retention**." That is a real, addressable, 24-hour-long copy of your prompts, on by default.
- Gemini paid tier: data "may be stored **transiently or cached in any country** in which Google or its agents maintain facilities." **ZDR is not data residency.**

**CMEK doesn't save you either.** Google states outright that "prompt logs for the purposes of abuse monitoring are **not encrypted by Customer-managed encryption keys (CMEK)**." The one category of retained data you most care about is precisely the category your key does not cover.

### Who can technically see prompts

The strongest published employee-access claim from any major provider is AWS's **ZOA**: "no operators of the service can access model input or output." It is qualified by `aws_review` existing as a separate customer-instructed path.

Everyone else admits humans read prompts under defined conditions: Google ("authorized Google employees may assess the flagged content… via an internal governance assessment and review management platform"), Azure (SAWs + JIT manager approval, EEA-resident reviewers for EEA deployments), Bedrock (human review by AWS for flagged Fable 5 traffic), Anthropic ("a small set of approved reviewers," tamper-proof log), Cohere, and Mistral (as controller).

**Google's Access Transparency is the only customer-visible break-glass primitive** — and its [exclusions page](https://cloud.google.com/cloud-provider-access-management/access-transparency/docs/exclusions) contains the most important sentence in this report: logs are generated "**except in the following scenarios: Google is legally prohibited from notifying you of the access.**" The transparency log covers the honest-employee case and is silent by design in exactly the compelled-access case. Assume that is true everywhere, documented or not.

### Legal process

The escape hatch is in every document, nearly word-for-word: "unless required by law" (AWS, Cohere, Google, OpenAI), "as necessary to comply with… a binding order of a governmental body" (AWS §50.12.4), "except where needed to comply with law" (Anthropic ZDR).

Anthropic's [government-requests policy](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/9519291-what-is-anthropic-s-policy-for-handling-governmental-requests-for-user-information) (updated **March 16, 2026**) is above-average: disclosure only "in accordance with valid legal process (eg., a validly issued subpoena or warrant)"; notice to users "unless we believe we're legally prohibited from doing so"; and for enterprise customers, "we will generally ask the requester to contact our customer in the first instance."

**The CLOUD Act** (18 U.S.C. §2713) obliges US providers to produce data in their "possession, custody, or control" regardless of where it is stored. Every provider here except Mistral (France) and DeepSeek (PRC) is US-incorporated. EU residency commitments constrain *where bits sit*, never *who can be ordered to fetch them*. DeepSeek's PRC establishment applies the same logic in reverse.

**Practical consequence: ZDR reduces the volume available to compel; it is not a legal shield.** With nothing written, a subpoena still returns account identity, timestamps, token counts, IPs, billing records, and — for Anthropic — **safety classifier verdicts, which are a lossy but real derivative of your prompt content, indexed to your account, retained even under ZDR.**

⚠️ **Transparency reports don't answer this question anyway.** I could not retrieve current figures (Amazon 503, Anthropic/Microsoft JS-rendered, Google not reached). But structurally: **none of these reports break out "enterprise API customer prompt content" as a category.** A low number is evidence the report doesn't measure that, not evidence prompts aren't compelled.

### SOC 2 / ISO — what they actually attest

Bluntly: **process audits, not data-handling guarantees.**

- **SOC 2 Type II** attests that an organization's *self-selected* controls, in a *self-written* system description, over a *self-scoped* set of Trust Services Criteria, operated effectively during a window. Security is the only mandatory criterion. A clean SOC 2 is entirely compatible with "we retain all prompts 30 days and employees read flagged ones." The report is under NDA, so a trust-center badge you haven't read the report behind tells you essentially nothing about retention. (OpenAI's portal, [trust.openai.com](https://trust.openai.com/), lists SOC 2 Type 2 covering July 1, 2025 – June 30, 2026, plus ISO 27001:2022 and ISO 42001:2023.)
- **ISO 27001** certifies an ISMS exists within a scope *you* define in the Statement of Applicability. That scope can be narrow.
- **ISO 42001** is closer in shape — AI governance, impact assessment, lifecycle controls — but it is still a *management-system* certification. It attests you have a process for deciding things, not what you decided.

Google makes the conflation for you, citing its training restriction as "validated by independent auditors against international standards (SOC 1/2/3, ISO 27001, etc.)." What auditors validated is that Google has controls *asserting* the restriction.

**The only technically-enforced retention control found anywhere: an AWS SCP on `bedrock:DataRetentionMode`.** Everything else is a promise.

### Leak paths ZDR does not close

1. **Abuse/safety logging** — the #1 path, on by default nearly everywhere.
2. **Safety classifier outputs** — retained by Anthropic *under ZDR*.
3. **Default-on `store=true`** — Google Interactions API, Fireworks Responses API, Bedrock `default` mode. Three vendors shipped OpenAI-compatible Responses APIs with storage on by default.
4. **Prompt caching** — server-side copies keyed to a prefix hash, everywhere.
5. **Grounding/search/tool subsystems** — Google stores Grounding-with-Search data 30d (Gemini API) / 3d (Vertex) and Maps 30d, **with no way to disable**, and the Search clause "will survive termination of the Agreement." Anthropic's BAA "would not apply to use of the web search functionality." **Any tool your agent calls is a separate, usually weaker, data-handling regime than the model.**
6. **Crash reports and error traces** — Gemini's controller-terms list explicitly includes "software errors and **crash reports**." Stack traces routinely carry request fragments.
7. **Cross-region movement** — Bedrock retains in the *destination* Region of cross-region inference.
8. **Subprocessors** — ⚠️ I could not retrieve current lists (Anthropic 404, OpenAI 403). Structurally: Anthropic's own model card lists "cloud computing resources from Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud Platform and Microsoft Azure," so **your prompt to Anthropic transits hyperscaler infrastructure regardless of which endpoint you called.**

⚠️ **2025–26 incidents involving exposed or compelled prompt data: not researched.** Search budget ran out first. I will not assert breaches from memory.

# 3. Confidential inference actually purchasable in 2026

The market splits sharply: one mass-market product with real client verification, a small tier of genuinely-verifiable startups, hyperscalers selling substrate rather than service, and two frontier labs selling nothing.

**The single most useful resource:** [confidentialinference.net](https://confidentialinference.net/) continuously challenges providers' attestation endpoints through six gates (Hardware+TCB → Freshness → Confidential Channel → Workload Identity → Source Provenance → Request Route). **The scoreboard: out of 82 checks, 30 passed hardware+TCB, 13 explicitly failed, 20 incomplete, 48 unavailable — and exactly 1 achieved full end-to-end verification.** Lead with that number whenever anyone pitches you "verifiable AI." ([attestation monitor](https://confidentialinference.net/attestation))

### Apple Private Cloud Compute — GA to third-party developers, hard gates

⚠️ **This contradicts a common belief and a peer agent's assertion, so here is the evidence.** The Foundation Models framework now ships [`PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/privatecloudcomputelanguagemodel) with a [dedicated guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute) and an [entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.private-cloud-compute), introduced at iOS/macOS 27. Developers send arbitrary prompts via the same `LanguageModelSession.respond` API ("You change a single line of code"), against Apple's server model at 32K context.

**The gates** ([developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/](https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/)): App Store Small Business Program enrollment, **fewer than 2 million first-time downloads**, and a granted entitlement. Cost is **$0** — but quota belongs to the *end user*, who gets "a daily request limit" and can buy more via **iCloud+**. This is a free tier for small App Store apps, not a general inference API.

**Verification is the strongest published:** "The user's device will not send data to any PCC nodes if it cannot validate their certificates" ([security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/)) — the OS checks before the prompt leaves, backed by an append-only transparency log, published build images, a [Virtual Research Environment](https://security.apple.com/blog/pcc-security-research/), partial source at `github.com/apple/security-pcc`, and $1M bounties.

**2026 caveats:** PCC now runs partly on Google Cloud ([security.apple.com/blog/expanding-pcc/](https://security.apple.com/blog/expanding-pcc/)); the [SOC 3 audit](https://support.apple.com/guide/certifications/apple-private-cloud-compute-soc-3-audit-apc95a31b9d8/web) covers only the *provisioning system* and explicitly "was not conducted to evaluate the performance or integrity of Apple's artificial intelligence services"; and **CVE-2026-20685** (Zip-Slip in `darwin-init`) punctured sealed observability, exposing request metadata — $150k bounty ([writeup](https://blog.sentry.security/beyond-prompt-injection-hacking-apples-private-cloud-compute/)). What you still trust: Apple, entirely — plus Google Cloud as operator. No reproducible build from public source.

### Anthropic and OpenAI: nothing to buy

**Anthropic** has one artifact, [Confidential Inference via Trusted VMs](https://www.anthropic.com/research/confidential-inference-trusted-vms) (June 2025), which says of itself: "This is just a sketch of our research… it is **too soon to forecast** how it will evolve into specific designs or features." No 2025–26 product announcement. (Their Aug 2026 "Model Hardware Standard" is a lab-device driver spec, unrelated despite the name.) **OpenAI:** no purchasable offering found; openai.com 403'd every fetch, so flagged as unverified-absence rather than confirmed-absence — but the independent directory doesn't list them either.

**Do not buy anything on the strength of either lab's research posts.**

### Hyperscalers: substrate GA, managed service never attested

- **Azure:** [DCesv6](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/virtual-machines/sizes/general-purpose/dcesv6-series) (Intel TDX) GA; [NCC40ads_H100_v5](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/virtual-machines/sizes/gpu-accelerated/nccadsh100v5-series) with "TEE spans confidential VM on the CPU and attached GPU" — **single GPU only**. [Confidential Clean Rooms](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/confidential-computing/confidential-clean-rooms) is **limited preview**, "shouldn't be used in production," and **inference is not in the preview**. Azure's own [confidential-ai page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/confidential-computing/confidential-ai) is unmaintained (still says A100 limited preview, still lists the defunct Mithril Security).
- **Google Cloud** has the best GA substrate: [Confidential VM](https://docs.cloud.google.com/confidential-computing/confidential-vm/docs/supported-configurations) with SEV/SEV-SNP/TDX and **NVIDIA CC on A3 High (H100) and G4**; [Confidential Space](https://docs.cloud.google.com/confidential-computing/confidential-space/docs/confidential-space-overview) GA — the real multi-party primitive, where a data owner releases keys only to an attested workload; Confidential GKE Nodes GA. [Private AI Compute](https://blog.google/technology/ai/google-private-ai-compute/) (Gemini on TPUs in Titanium Intelligence Enclaves) is **consumer-only, not exposed to developers**. "Confidential Vertex AI" does not appear to exist.
- **AWS is the outlier — strong claims, zero attestation of managed inference.** Nitro Enclaves give real client-verifiable attestation **of your own enclave image**. But the Nitro operator-isolation claim ("no mechanism for any system or person to log in to EC2 servers, read the memory of EC2 instances") is an *assertion audited by NCC Group in 2022*, not a quote you can challenge with a nonce. **Bedrock is not attested to the client — confirmed:** [data-protection.html](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html) contains no mention of TEEs, enclaves, or attestation. Its strongest privacy claim is organizational: Model Deployment Accounts mean providers "don't have access to Amazon Bedrock logs or to customer prompts and completions" — **that protects you from Anthropic, not from AWS.**

**Summary: you can rent an attestable confidential TDX+H100/B200 VM and attest your own stack. No hyperscaler lets you attest their managed LLM endpoint.**

### The TEE-native tier

**Tinfoil** ([tinfoil.sh](https://tinfoil.sh/)) is the most rigorous and the only provider at full end-to-end verification in the independent monitor. GA, SOC 2. Models (open weights only): Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Gemma 4 31B, GPT-OSS 120B, Llama 3.3 70B, plus vision/audio/embedding. Attestation is deep — SEV-SNP and TDX guest quotes with a [published predicate format](https://docs.tinfoil.sh/verification/predicate.md), a purpose-built CVM image (dm-verity root, no shell, no package manager, module loading disabled after GPU driver init), **model weights themselves dm-verity-verified against a Merkle root**, and GPU CC mode checked via NVIDIA's `local-gpu-verifier` at boot. The client path is the good version: SDKs fetch expected measurements from GitHub + Sigstore, compare against the live quote, and only then open the encrypted channel — with **byte-for-byte reproducible Nix builds**, so "auditors can trace the measurement… back to public source code, **without trusting Tinfoil's build machines**." Pricing: $20/mo chat; API ~$0.05/1M in; containers $20/mo + usage. Still trusted: CPU vendor root key, **GitHub CI**, Sigstore, OVMF, NVIDIA.

**Privatemode** ([privatemode.ai](https://www.privatemode.ai/)) — note **Edgeless Systems' "Continuum AI" is gone**; the URL 308-redirects here. GA, EU-hosted, ISO 27001, with published prices (GLM-5.3 €1.55/€7.74 per 1M; gpt-oss-120b €0.43/€1.70). Monitor grades it "incomplete," not end-to-end verified. **Price premium for confidential open-weights inference: roughly 2–4× commodity hosting.**

**Phala** ([phala.com/pricing](https://phala.com/pricing)): CVM from $0.06/hr, **GPU TEE from $3.80/hr** — the cheapest way to rent GPU TEE by the hour. **Venice.ai** launched E2EE in March 2026 but **resells NEAR AI and Phala** and shows **10 explicit attestation failures with workload hash mismatches** — the exact gap between showing you a report and matching a published build. **NEAR AI, NanoGPT, Chutes, Redpill, PPQ** are live at $0.01–0.16/1M with largely unavailable or failing verification. **OpenPCC** ([github.com/openpcc/openpcc](https://github.com/openpcc/openpcc), Apache-2.0) is an open re-implementation of PCC's design.

⚠️ **Could not verify in 2026:** Opaque, Fortanix, Nillion, Super Protocol, Marlin/Oyster, Atoma, Decentriq, Anjuna. **Mithril Security / BlindLlama: treat as defunct** (last activity 2023). **Secret Network: actively compromised** — consensus seeds extracted from its SGX deployment.

### NVIDIA Confidential Computing: the performance question is settled

The best 2026 measurement is [arXiv:2608.26575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26575) (Confidential.ai, June 2026) — paired CC-on/CC-off runs on one host, 8× B200, TDX SEAM 2.0.14, changing only the CC bit:

- **~1–3% throughput overhead for confidential inference when configured correctly** (MiniMax-M2.7 229B MoE on 4× B200: 1.5%).
- **"Stock inference stacks incur 30 to 40% penalties due to avoidable configurations"** — CC forces synchronous per-step token readback, serializing the scheduler; full CUDA graphs + CC patches bring it under 1%.
- **Training is the real tax: 8-GPU CC training ≈ 10–13%.**
- NVLink encryption: bandwidth −11 to −12%; small cross-GPU write latency **14.5 µs vs 3.7 µs (~4×)**. Fixed **700 MB VRAM carveout** on B200.
- **GPUDirect RDMA is blocked outright under GPU CC** — so prefill/decode disaggregation is an unsolved integration problem. CUDA-event timing is disabled by design, silently breaking kernel autotuners.
- The paper is explicit: "**It does not audit any security property.**"

**Plan for 30–40% until you've done the configuration work, not 1–3%.**

### The part that should change how you read all of the above

**TEE attestation was broken in 2025–26 by the exact adversary the category exists to exclude.**

- **Battering RAM** ([batteringram.eu](https://batteringram.eu/), IEEE S&P 2026, Pwnie Award Aug 2026): a **~$50** DDR4 interposer, transparent at boot, active at runtime. Breaks **SGX and AMD SEV-SNP** for read, write, replay and plaintext. Critically, it **defeats SEV-SNP's launch measurement by replaying captured digests** — it breaks *attestation*, not just confidentiality. **Intel TDX marked not affected.** No software or firmware patch exists; the fix "would require fundamental redesign of memory encryption itself." ([Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/news/more-information-encrypted-memory-frameworks.html), [AMD](https://www.amd.com/en/resources/product-security/bulletin/amd-sb-3024.html))
- **TEE.fail** ([tee.fail](https://tee.fail/), Oct 2025): DDR5 interposition, **under $1,000**, off-the-shelf. **Extracted Intel TDX/SGX provisioning certification keys → forged attestations.** Recovered AMD SEV-SNP ECDSA signing keys even with Ciphertext Hiding. And the finding that matters most here: "**NVIDIA CC attestations aren't bound to specific CVMs, allowing attackers to relay authentic GPU attestations**" — you can borrow a genuine GPU attestation while running the workload unencrypted.
- **XCA** ([xca-attacks.github.io](https://xca-attacks.github.io/overview/), May 2026): attacks AMD Infinity Fabric routing to undermine SEV-SNP's PSP root of trust.

**What this means, plainly:** confidential inference exists to exclude the cloud operator. These attacks are executed by someone who can touch the DIMM slot — which *is* the operator. Both vendors classify physical access as out of scope. **The threat model and the product pitch do not overlap.** Once attestation keys leak, a perfectly-verifying client accepts a quote from hardware that is protecting nothing. Every client-side verification path above, Tinfoil's included, sits on top of this. Intel TDX currently looks better than SEV-SNP, which is consistent with TDX being the substrate of choice for the serious vendors.

# 4. FHE and MPC: not usable, not close

**Zama does not do what people think.** Concrete ML's LLM path ([docs.zama.org/concrete-ml/llms](https://docs.zama.org/concrete-ml/llms)) is `HybridFHEModel` — an explicitly *partial* scheme where the server runs only linear layers encrypted, while **the attention mechanism and activations run in the clear on the client**. That is model-IP protection plus partial data hiding, not confidential inference. Measured: GPT-2 at **~11 s/token on GPU, ~300 s/token on CPU**; Llama-3.2-1B at **~18 MB transferred per token**. Documented examples are GPT-2 and Llama-3.2-1B only. And **Zama has largely left ML** — their 2025–26 blog is almost entirely confidential blockchain; zero LLM posts in 2026.

**The current real numbers:**

| System | Model | Measurement | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Odin** ([2609.12378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12378), Sep 2026) | Llama-3-8B, all 32 layers | **366.4 s for a 128-token prefill** — prefill only, no generation | 1× H100 |
| **Zama/CryptoLab** ([2601.18511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18511)) | Llama-3-8B | **18 s/token** generation | **8× RTX PRO 6000** |
| **Cachemir** ([2602.11470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11470)) | Llama-3-8B + KV cache | **<100 s/token** | 1 GPU |
| **PUMA** (3-party MPC, [2307.12533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12533)) | LLaMA-7B | **~300 s/token**; 2026 papers claim 10–20× → ~15–30 s/token, plus GB-scale communication per inference | — |

**Slowdown factor: 3–4 orders of magnitude (~10⁴×)**, down from ~10⁵–10⁶ in 2023–24. Improving roughly 10×/year, entirely from algorithms — *not* from FHE silicon.

**Make it concrete.** One agentic coding turn is ~50k context tokens and ~2k output tokens. At 18 s/token that is **~10 hours per turn on eight $8k GPUs**, and an agent does dozens of turns. Even a further 1000× improvement lands you at today's plaintext single-GPU speed for an 8B model — a model two orders of magnitude too small to be a useful coding agent. **No published FHE result exists for any frontier-scale model**; everything above is Llama-3-8B or smaller.

**The hardware story got worse, not better.** DARPA **DPRIVE is complete** — its [program page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/data-protection-in-virtual-environments) now reads "This program is now complete… no longer maintained," with no claim its one-order-of-magnitude goal was met. **Optalysys has pivoted off FHE** to silicon-photonic interconnect. **Fabric Cryptography** offers pre-order only, no timeline, no quantified FHE benchmark. **Cornami** is the only one with concrete product (Tigris ASIC, MX64 rack, "up to 80× faster homomorphic matrix multiplication") — 80× against a 10⁴ gap is not sufficient. **Niobium and Chain Reaction: domains do not resolve; status unconfirmed.** The tell: every credible 2026 LLM-FHE number was produced on **stock NVIDIA GPUs**, not an FHE ASIC.

⚠️ **Watch for a paper that will be misquoted:** [arXiv 2604.12168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12168) claims 237 ms latency and 80 tok/s for "FHE on Llama 3" — it secures only "**some of its layers**." Not end-to-end private inference.

**The adjacent techniques people confuse with this:** *Differential privacy* protects the training set against extraction; the server still sees your plaintext prompt. *Secure aggregation* hides individual gradient updates in federated training; inference is untouched. *Split inference* (run N layers on-device, ship activations) **leaks** — intermediate hidden states invert back to input text with high fidelity. All three are training-time or partial constructs offered as inference-time substitutes. They aren't.

**Verdict: use a TEE or self-host. FHE/MPC needs three to four more years at the current rate *plus* a frontier-scale demonstration before "interactive" is even arguable.**

### Hardware, concretely

**~1T+ class (Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4-Pro).** Per [Unsloth's guides](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/kimi-k3.md): Kimi K3 needs **610 GB** at UD-IQ1_S (594 GB, ~78.9% top-1 retained) or **1,600 GB** at Q8_0; "~20 tokens/s generation… >120 tokens/s throughput" on B200s. That means an 8×H200 or 8×B200 node — **$250k–400k capex or ~$25–50/hr rented**. Against DeepSeek's own API at **$1.32/$3.96 per MTok** peak, $0.66/$1.98 off-peak, **self-hosting this class is economically irrational unless you're saturating the node or legally required to.**

**100–450B class — the sweet spot, and it changed in 2026.** GLM-5.3-Flash: **199.71 GB at UD-Q4_K_XL (92.22% accuracy retained)**, 120.37 GB at IQ3_XXS (81.63%). DeepSeek-V4-Flash: **155–162 GB at Q4_K_XL**, near-lossless (wikitext-2 PPL 4.5335 vs 4.5319 at Q8; KL-div 0.0102), hitting **120 tok/s on a B200** with DSpark speculative decoding.

The best price/capability point for a self-hosted agentic coder in 2026: **2× RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell 96GB = 192 GB, ~$17k.** A 512GB Mac Studio fits Q4 via MLX but ~800 GB/s bandwidth and slow prefill make it poor for agentic prompts. DGX Spark (128 GB) fits only the **81.6%-retention** tier.

**30B class:** Qwen3.8-27B (Apache 2.0) runs 4-bit in **16–19 GB** — a single RTX 4090/5080 — and scores SWE-bench Pro 61.7, Terminal-Bench 2.1 73.0, LiveCodeBench 90.3.

Stack: vLLM has first-class classes for all of them; SGLang, llama.cpp, MLX, Ollama, LM Studio all supported (GLM-5.3-Flash alone has 109 community quants).

### The tradeoffs that actually bite an agent

- **Throughput collapses exactly where agents live.** GLM-5.3-Flash on a single B200: **58.6 tok/s at 4k context → 20.66 at 64k** — 2.8× degradation, *with* a q4-quantized KV cache. Speculative decoding recovers 1.5–2× and is effectively mandatory.
- **Prompt caching is the biggest under-discussed gap.** Anthropic charges **10% of base for cache reads (2.5% on Fable 5.1)**; DeepSeek charges **$0.022 vs $0.66 hit-vs-miss — 30×**. Self-hosted prefix caching works but is per-instance, evicts under load, and earns you no billing benefit — you pay the GPU-hour regardless. For an agent replaying a 50k-token system prompt every turn, this is a structural cost advantage self-hosting cannot reproduce.
- **The quantization tax is real at sizes that fit real hardware.** Published benchmarks are BF16 scores. The 128 GB tier that fits a Mac or DGX Spark is the **81.6%-retention** tier. What you run at home is not the model that was benchmarked.
- **Context is no longer a differentiator** — DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and Kimi K3 both ship 1M, matching Claude Fable 5.1 / Opus 5 / Sonnet 5.
- **"Open models can't call tools reliably" is obsolete.** Toolathlon Verified 76.5 and AutomationBench 54.8 are best-in-table, beating every hosted frontier model.
