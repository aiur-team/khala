# Cross-agent prompt injection: state of play, September 2026

Framing for your case: a peer agent from another company whose messages land in your agent's context is, in every framework below, an **untrusted content channel** ("red" in MCP Colors, [A] in Meta's Rule of Two, leg 2 of the lethal trifecta). Nothing in 2025–2026 changes that classification; what changed is that the labs now say so in writing, the attacks are operational, and the only defenses with a security argument are structural (remove capabilities, constrain the action space, taint-track), not detective.

Verification note: everything below was retrieved from the web on 2026-09-16. Items marked [unverified] rest on search-engine summaries only.

---

## 1. Is it solvable? Current consensus

**Consensus: no general solution; treat the model as unable to separate instruction from data, and design around that.** Every primary source now says this explicitly:

- Willison, [The lethal trifecta](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/) (Jun 2025): "LLMs are unable to reliably distinguish the importance of instructions based on where they came from … we still don't know how to 100% reliably prevent this." Any two of {private data, untrusted content, external communication} are tolerable; all three are exploitable. Guardrail vendors claiming "95%" are dismissed as not security.
- Meta, [Agents Rule of Two](https://ai.meta.com/blog/practical-ai-agent-security/) (Oct 31 2025): "prompt injection is a fundamental, unsolved weakness in all LLMs." An agent may satisfy at most two of [A] process untrustworthy inputs, [B] access sensitive systems/private data, [C] change state or communicate externally; all three requires human oversight, not autonomy. Generalizes the trifecta beyond exfiltration to any harmful tool use. Summary: [Willison, Nov 2 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/2/new-prompt-injection-papers/).
- OpenAI, [Hardening Atlas against prompt injection](https://openai.com/index/hardening-atlas-against-prompt-injection/): "prompt injection, much like scams and social engineering on the web, is unlikely to ever be fully 'solved.'" OpenAI's June 2026 [Lockdown Mode](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/) is the operational admission: it blocks the exfiltration leg ("the final stage of data exfiltration") and explicitly does not stop injections from landing in context.
- Anthropic, [How we contain Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude): model-layer defenses "will never be 100% effective, which is why it can't stand alone"; "design for containment at the environment layer first, then steer behavior at the model layer"; "the software you build yourself is often the weakest" (prefer hypervisors/seccomp to custom guardrails).
- Google DeepMind, [Advancing Gemini's security safeguards](https://deepmind.google/blog/advancing-geminis-security-safeguards/) (May 2025): baseline mitigations "became much less effective against adaptive attacks"; "relying on defenses tested only against static defense approaches offers a false sense of security." Google's [layered defense post](https://blog.google/security/mitigating-prompt-injection-attacks/) (Jun 13 2025) lists five layers (injection classifiers, "security thought reinforcement," markdown/URL sanitization, user confirmation framework, end-user notifications) and publishes no efficacy numbers. Google's [in-the-wild scan](https://blog.google/security/prompt-injections-web/) (Apr 23 2026) found a 32% relative increase in malicious injections on Common Crawl between Nov 2025 and Feb 2026, "not yet productionized at scale."
- The adaptive-attack literature closes the argument. [The Attacker Moves Second](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023) (Nasr, Carlini, Tramèr et al., DeepMind/Anthropic/OpenAI/ETH, Oct 2025): 12 defenses reporting near-zero ASR driven to >90% (spotlighting ~1% -> >95%, sandwiching ~1% -> >95%, Meta SecAlign ~2% -> 96%, PromptGuard/Protect AI/Google Model Armor >90%, Circuit Breakers 100%); 500-person human red-team hit 100%. [Zhan et al. NAACL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00061): all 8 defenses on InjecAgent exceed 50% ASR under adaptive attack; fine-tuned detector 61% -> 1% detection rate.
- One genuinely new theoretical result: [Prompt Injection as Role Confusion](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/22/prompt-injection-as-role-confusion/) (Ye, Cui, Hadfield-Menell, Jun 2026): models infer role from *textual style*, not from actual role labels; "destyling" injected text drops ASR from 61% to 10%. Implication for your design: a `<peer_agent_message>` wrapper is only a weak signal; a peer that formats its message to look like your system prompt or your own reasoning trace gets treated accordingly. "Unless LLMs achieve genuine role perception" it stays an arms race.

**Counter-evidence that models are getting hard to break with commodity attacks:** [hackmyclaw](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/26/hack-my-ai-assistant/) (Jun 2026): 2,000 people, 6,000 attempts, $500 of tokens against Opus 4.6 with anti-injection rules, zero secret leaks. Anthropic's Trajectory Labs eval: 0/720 attempts succeeded against Fable 5/Opus 5/Sonnet 5 in auto mode ([Willison, Aug 8 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/8/auto-mode/)). Gray Swan IPI Arena (Mar 2026): Claude Opus 4.5 0.5% per-attempt ASR ([arXiv:2603.15714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15714)). But Anthropic's own [Opus 4.5 system card](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/64823ba7485345a7/Claude-Opus-4-5-System-Card.pdf) shows 0.3% at 1 attempt becoming 10% at 200 attempts in coding, and Sonnet 4.5 87.5% at 200; break curves in the arena are linear in attempts. Per-attempt robustness buys attacker cost, not immunity. Willison's stance: "the 6,000 failures don't guarantee immunity" and don't deploy where injection can cause irreversible damage without additional safeguards.

### The design-patterns paper and CaMeL

[Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08837) (Beurer-Kellner, Debenedetti, Tramèr, Fabian, Paverd et al., Jun 2025; [Willison summary](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/13/prompt-injection-design-patterns/); [code samples](https://github.com/ReversecLabs/design-patterns-for-securing-llm-agents-code-samples)). Governing principle: "once an LLM agent has ingested untrusted input, it must be constrained so that it is impossible for that input to trigger any consequential actions." The authors state outright that general-purpose agents with meaningful guarantees are unlikely with current LLMs.

| Pattern | Mechanism | Applied to your peer-agent channel | Status | Known gap ([ARMO critique](https://www.armosec.io/blog/design-patterns-for-securing-llm-agents/)) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Action-selector | LLM picks from a fixed action list; never sees tool output | Peer message can only select among pre-approved responses | Production-viable for narrow bots | Can still steer toward the wrong allow-listed action |
| Plan-then-execute | Commit tool-call plan before reading untrusted content | Decide what to do with the peer's message before reading it | Emerging | Arguments (recipients, bodies) remain attacker-influenceable |
| LLM map-reduce | Isolated sub-agents return only constrained/structured outputs | Quarantined worker summarizes peer message into a fixed schema | Emerging; the most practical for your case | Attacker can still bias the structured output (e.g. ranking) |
| Dual LLM | Privileged LLM never sees untrusted text; quarantined LLM's output referenced via symbolic variables | Privileged orchestrator handles `$peer_msg_1` as an opaque token | Research/rare in production | Quarantined output propagates attacker-shaped content when dereferenced |
| Code-then-execute (CaMeL) | Privileged LLM emits code in a DSL; interpreter taint-tracks data flow with capabilities | Peer content carries a "from: peer-corp" capability; policy forbids it reaching `send_email(to=external)` | Research-only | Injection viable if it survives into generated code; policy authoring burden |
| Context-minimization | Strip untrusted content from context after it's been converted | Drop raw peer text after extracting structured fields | Production-viable, cheap | Can be induced to omit critical info |

ARMO's summary is the sharpest one-liner: all six lock *which* action runs (the verb) but leave *what values it carries* (the object) open.

**CaMeL** ([Defeating Prompt Injections by Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813), DeepMind/ETH, Mar 2025 rev. Jun 2025; [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/11/camel/)): 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security vs 84% undefended. It is the only approach whose claim is not an empirical ASR and therefore cannot be eroded by adaptive attack. Limits, stated in the paper's §8.3: users must write and maintain security policies; approval prompts risk "a fugue state where they say 'yes' to everything." Follow-on: [CaMeLs can use computers too](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09923) (2026). Classification: **research-only**, no known production deployment.

**Classification for section 1:** trifecta / Rule of Two framing: **production-ready mental model, universally adopted**. Design patterns: **emerging** (map-reduce, context-minimization, action-selector are cheap and shipping in bespoke systems). CaMeL/dual-LLM: **research-only**. Model-level hardening: **production, but explicitly probabilistic**.

## 2. Provenance / trust labelling of content

Short version: **no shipping protocol carries a normative trust or provenance label on message content.** Every proposal is either app-level metadata convention, a prompt-formatting trick, or training.

**MCP (spec 2026-07-28).** I read the current spec directly.
- [Security best practices](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/security_best_practices) is entirely OAuth/infra: confused deputy, token passthrough, SSRF, state-handle hijacking, local server compromise, mix-up attacks, localhost redirect impersonation, CIMD trust policies, scope minimization. Zero content on prompt injection or content provenance.
- [Tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/server/tools): "there SHOULD always be a human in the loop with the ability to deny tool invocations"; "clients MUST consider tool annotations to be untrusted unless they come from trusted servers"; clients SHOULD "validate tool results before passing to LLM." All SHOULD-level; no wire-level trust field on `content`.
- [Base protocol](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/index): `clientInfo`/`serverInfo` "are self-reported by the sender and are not verified by the protocol … SHOULD NOT rely on them for security decisions." `_meta` with reverse-DNS prefixes is the only extension point for custom trust labels.
- Anthropic declined to change MCP STDIO configuration-as-execution semantics after OX Security's April 2026 advisory (11 downstream CVEs across LiteLLM, LangChain, Flowise), calling it "expected" ([THN](https://thehackernews.com/2026/04/anthropic-mcp-design-vulnerability.html), [OX](https://www.ox.security/blog/mcp-supply-chain-advisory-rce-vulnerabilities-across-the-ai-ecosystem/)). NSA published an MCP security CSI in May 2026 ([PDF](https://www.nsa.gov/Portals/75/documents/Cybersecurity/CSI_MCP_SECURITY.pdf)). Academic: [Breaking the Protocol](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17549) (Jan 2026).
- Classification: **production protocol; provenance labelling aspirational-with-no-adoption at spec level.**

**A2A (Linux Foundation, v1.0 in 2026).** [What is A2A](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/topics/what-is-a2a/) and [Enterprise-ready](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/topics/enterprise-ready/) cover TLS, auth headers, authz, tracing. The spec's own framing is "agents can't see the inner workings of the agents they collaborate with" (opacity as a feature). **Nothing on prompt injection, untrusted peer output, or content trust labels.** Agent cards are self-asserted and unsigned by default. Demonstrated attacks: Agent Card Poisoning ([Keysight, Mar 12 2026](https://www.keysight.com/blogs/en/tech/nwvs/2026/03/12/agent-card-poisoning)) where `description`/`skills` fields carry instructions the orchestrator embeds verbatim in planning context; concrete instance A2A-2026-001 in [langgraph-a2a-client](https://github.com/5enxia/langgraph-a2a-client/issues/8); "Agent in the Middle" ([Trustwave SpiderLabs](https://www.levelblue.com/blogs/spiderlabs-blog/agent-in-the-middle-abusing-agent-cards-in-the-agent-2-agent-protocol-to-win-all-the-tasks)) where inflated cards win task routing. Academic proposals ([arXiv:2505.12490](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12490), [arXiv:2506.23260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23260)) recommend "dynamic trust management, cryptographic provenance tracking, sandboxed agent interfaces" — none standardized. Classification: **A2A production protocol; injection model absent; every cross-agent trust proposal research-only.** For your scenario this is the decisive fact: **A2A gives you authentication of the peer, never trust of the peer's content.** Authenticated ≠ trusted; the peer can be honest-but-compromised (its own upstream got injected) and forward the payload with a valid signature.

**MCP Colors** ([Tim Kellogg, Nov 3 2025](https://timkellogg.me/blog/2025/11/03/colors); [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/4/mcp-colors/)): label every tool/input red (exposes untrusted content) or blue (consequential action); an agent session may hold red or blue but not both; labels stored in MCP `_meta`; LLM-assisted bulk labelling of ~200 tools. Extensions: 1-5 trust levels, "de-coloring" via review. Classification: **emerging convention, no standard, no known vendor adoption.** It is the practical form of the Rule of Two for tool inventories and is the thing I would actually implement for a peer-agent channel (peer channel = red; any state-changing tool = blue; never both in one context).

**OpenAI instruction hierarchy** ([paper](https://openai.com/index/the-instruction-hierarchy/), Apr 2024, older; [2025 update](https://openai.com/index/instruction-hierarchy-challenge/)): trained priority system > developer > user > tool. Shipped in GPT-4o-mini onward and GPT-5 family; only usable public number is a robustness score 0.963 in the GPT-5.5 system card (Apr 2026, down from 0.998 for GPT-5.4-thinking), one surface, known attacks only ([VentureBeat comparison](https://venturebeat.com/security/anthropic-browser-agent-hijacked-31-percent-before-safeguards-engaged)). A 2026 paper proposes many-tier hierarchies for agents ([arXiv:2604.09443](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09443v2)). Relevant to you: **there is no "peer agent" tier.** A peer's message arrives at tool-result privilege at best, and the role-confusion result says the model keys on style anyway. Classification: **production, probabilistic, single-vendor.**

**Microsoft Spotlighting** (delimiting / datamarking / encoding of untrusted spans; [MSRC July 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/blog/2025/07/how-microsoft-defends-against-indirect-prompt-injection-attacks), [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/defend-indirect-prompt-injection), [MCP-specific](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/protecting-against-indirect-injection-attacks-mcp/)): in production inside Prompt Shields. Independent numbers: AgentDojo `spotlighting_with_delimiting` cut GPT-4o ASR only 47.7% -> 41.7%; Nasr et al. drove it from ~1% to >95%. Classification: **production, cheap, near-zero security value under adaptive attack; still worth doing as a hygiene marker for your own prompt engineering.**

**Anthropic, Claude Code** — the one real cross-agent provenance control I found shipping ([permission-modes docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)): in auto mode "the classifier also reviews each message Claude sends to another agent with SendMessage … before Claude Code delivers it" (v2.1.222+); subagent reports are classifier-reviewed before the parent reads them and delivered "prepended with a security warning" if flagged; and crucially "tool results are stripped from [classifier] requests, so hostile content in a file or web page can't manipulate the classifier directly." That last line is a shipped dual-LLM-lite: the judge never sees the untrusted bytes. Also `PostToolUse` hooks can attach `classifierContext` annotations to a result — effectively an app-level provenance tag. Classification: **production, single vendor, intra-org agents only; no equivalent for cross-company peers.**

**Older/adjacent:** [Prompt Infection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07283) (Lee & Tiwari, Oct 2024, stale) proposed "LLM Tagging" (prefix every agent-generated message with its origin) and reported it "significantly" reduces multi-agent spread; no numbers in abstract, no adoption. [Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02077) (Schroeder de Witt et al., rev. Apr 2026) frames network-effect propagation of jailbreaks/poisoning but offers agenda, not mechanism.

## 3. Human confirmation gates: what ships, and whether humans catch anything

**What ships**
- Claude Code ([security docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/security)): Manual mode = read-only default, prompt per write/exec/network; allowlists for prompt-fatigue mitigation; fail-closed matching; command-injection detection re-prompts even on allowlisted commands; isolated context window for web fetch; sandbox with filesystem + network isolation (84% fewer prompts internally, [Anthropic engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-sandboxing), [sandbox-runtime](https://github.com/anthropic-experimental/sandbox-runtime)). **Auto mode is now the default on Pro/Max/Team** (Aug 8 2026): a separate classifier reviews actions instead of the human; explicit `ask` rules still force prompts; after 3 consecutive or 20 total blocks it falls back to prompting. Docs' own residual-risk line: "no system is completely immune to all attacks."
- Google Gemini Workspace: "user confirmation framework" for risky actions (calendar deletion etc.) ([blog](https://blog.google/security/mitigating-prompt-injection-attacks/)); Chrome added layered defenses Dec 2025 ([THN](https://thehackernews.com/2025/12/google-adds-layered-defenses-to-chrome.html)).
- OpenAI ChatGPT agent/Operator: Watch Mode, takeover, confirmations (page 403'd to me; secondary coverage in [Cyberscoop](https://cyberscoop.com/openai-chatgpt-atlas-prompt-injection-browser-agent-security-update-head-of-preparedness/)). Lockdown Mode (Jun 2026) is the non-confirmation alternative: cut egress.
- Claude for Chrome ([Aug 2025 -> GA Dec 2025](https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-chrome)): site allow/blocklists, action confirmations; red-team 123 cases/29 scenarios: 23.6% -> 11.2% ASR after mitigations; browser-specific set 35.7% -> 0%.
- MCP spec: human-in-loop is SHOULD, not MUST (see §2).

**Does the human catch anything? The 2026 evidence says essentially no.**
- Anthropic's controlled study (reported in [Willison, Aug 8 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/8/auto-mode/) and [How we contain Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude)): 1,053 paid developers; a harmful action was swapped into a permission prompt mid-session; **only 13.6% refused**. Auto mode's classifier would have blocked 89% (elsewhere "~83% of overeager behaviors"). This is the single most important number for your design: **a human confirmation gate on agent actions catches roughly one in seven attacks.** Anthropic's response was to replace the human with a classifier by default.
- The classifier is also breakable: Rehberger's [Breaking Claude Code Opus 5 Auto Mode](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/27/breaking-claude-code-opus-5-auto-mode/) (Aug 27 2026): zip extraction drops a malicious local `struct.py`, a later `import base64` executes it, ~80% success; and "Claude detects the compromise, but Auto Mode blocks its cleanup command." Willison's update notes this is a "confused environment" attack rather than the model following injected text. Recommendation from both: containers/VMs with restricted egress and no real credentials in the agent runtime.
- Habituation literature (pre-2025, flag as older but foundational): 50% of Chrome users clicked through SSL warnings in under 1.7s ([Bravo-Lillo et al., SOUPS 2014](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/soups14-paper-bravo-lillo.pdf)); fMRI shows visual-cortex response to a repeated warning drops after the second exposure ([Anderson et al., CHI 2015](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2702123.2702322)); polymorphic warnings resist habituation. 2026 application: [The approval prompt nobody reads](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/07/07/the-approval-prompt-nobody-reads). CaMeL's authors and Willison both name the "fugue state" problem. Willison's [Normalization of Deviance](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/10/normalization-of-deviance/) (Dec 2025) adds the organizational failure: absence of a successful attack is read as presence of security, so gates get removed.
- Confirmation gates also don't see what matters: EchoLeak, ShadowLeak, CamoLeak, ForcedLeak, AgentFlayer were all **zero-click** — the exfiltrating action was a "render an image" / "follow a link" step that no confirmation UX gates (§4).

**Classification:** confirmation gates: **production-ready as an audit/consent mechanism; aspirational as a security control** (13.6% catch rate). Classifier gates (Claude Code auto mode): **production, ~83-89% on measured harmful actions, already bypassed once at ~80%**. Sandboxing/egress control: **production and the only thing every source agrees on**.

Practical implication for a cross-company peer: gate on *irreversibility and egress*, not on "the peer said something." Make the rare gate polymorphic and specific (show the exact recipient/URL/diff), never a uniform "Approve?" on every tool call.

**Agent-to-agent specifically, and worms**
- Morris II (Cornell Tech/Technion, Mar 2024; **stale, PoC only**): self-replicating prompt through RAG-assisted email ([site](https://sites.google.com/view/compromptmized)).
- AI Worming through Word ([Måløy, Jul 29 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/29/ai-worming-through-word/)): hidden Word text instructs Copilot to copy the instructions into every generated document; 144-day disclosure window, no comprehensive Microsoft mitigation. First deliberately self-replicating injection in a shipping product.
- ClawWorm (U Toronto, Mar 2026, via [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-agentjacking-self-replicating-ai-worms-202/)): self-replicating worm against OpenClaw (40k+ instances), persists via config hijack, **propagates over peer agent messaging**, 64.5% success across 1,800 trials, no fix. This is your exact threat model realized.

## 5. Benchmarks and the actual numbers

**Headline: no defense reaches a usable number under adaptive attack; static leaderboards are saturated and stale; benchmarks don't even agree with each other** (Kendall's W = 0.10, p = 0.94 across 40 agent-safety benchmarks, [arXiv:2605.16282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16282)).

- **AgentDojo** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352), [results](https://agentdojo.spylab.ai/results/)): 97 tasks, 629 security cases, reports utility + utility-under-attack + targeted ASR. **Leaderboard frozen since 2025-02-24** (top entry Claude 3.7 Sonnet, 7.31% ASR; GPT-4-0125 56.28%). Defenses on GPT-4o: `transformers_pi_detector` 7.95% ASR but utility 69% -> 41%; `spotlighting_with_delimiting` 47.7% -> 41.7%; `tool_filter` 6.84% at 72% utility. 2026 third-party runs put Fable 5 / Gemini 3 Flash at 0% on a small banking subset ([arXiv:2607.05518](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05518v1)). Meta-SecAlign-70B 1.9%; GPT-5 (high) 0.2% ([arXiv:2507.02735](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02735v2)). Classification: **research-only / stale as a scoreboard.**
- **InjecAgent** ([arXiv:2403.02691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02691), 2024): GPT-4 ReAct 24% ASR, ~48% with reinforcement. Now floor-saturated (SecAlign 0.5%, Gemini Flash 2.5 0.1%). **Research-only, stale.**
- **Agent Security Bench** ([arXiv:2410.02644](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02644v4)): 13 backbones, 400+ tools. Avg ASR: DPI 72.7%, IPI 27.6%, memory poisoning 7.9%, mixed 84.3%. Defenses barely move IPI: delimiters 28.0 -> 25.0, sandwich 28.0 -> 28.0 (worse); only instructional prevention moves DPI (78 -> 57). **Emerging/aging; best hard evidence that prompt-level defenses do nothing.**
- **Gray Swan IPI Arena** ([arXiv:2603.15714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15714), Mar 2026, with OpenAI/Anthropic/Meta/UK AISI/US CAISI): 464 red-teamers, 271k chats, 41 scenarios (tool use, coding on real repos, computer use), dual objective (do harm AND conceal). Per-attempt ASR: Gemini 2.5 Pro 8.5%, SecAlign-70B 5.5%, GPT-5 2.0%, Claude Sonnet 4.5 1.0%, **Claude Opus 4.5 0.5%**. Every model broken; Gemini/DeepSeek 100% scenario coverage, Opus 4.5 35%. Breaks grow **linearly** with attempts. Capability doesn't predict robustness (r = -0.31, n.s.). Quarterly refresh committed. **Production-ready; the benchmark to cite.**
- **ART** ([arXiv:2507.20526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20526)): 1.8M attacks, 22 agents: "nearly all agents exhibit policy violations for most behaviors within 10–100 queries." Used in Anthropic system cards. **Production-ready.**
- **WASP** (Meta browser agents, [arXiv:2504.18575](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18575v2)): ASR-intermediate (agent starts following) vs end-to-end: o1 85.7% vs 16.7%; Claude 3.5 58.3% vs 6.0%. The gap is agent incompetence, not security; treat the 85.7% as the signal. **Emerging.**
- **b³ / threat snapshots** (Lakera + UK AISI, ICLR 2026, [arXiv:2510.22620](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22620v2)): 194k crowdsourced attacks, 34 LLMs; reasoning helps, size doesn't. **Emerging.**
- **MCP-specific**: tool-description poisoning near 100% ASR on GPT-4o ([arXiv:2605.24069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24069)); ShareLock multi-tool secret-sharing >90% ([arXiv:2606.27027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27027)). **Research-only, alarming.**
- **Adaptive attacks** (the numbers that matter): Zhan et al. NAACL 2025 — all 8 defenses >50% ASR; detectors 61% -> 1% detection; perplexity filter 24% -> 76% ASR. Nasr et al. — table in §1. **DeepMind's own paper** ([arXiv:2505.14534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14534)): undefended Gemini 2.0 ~100% ASR under TAP for <$10; **hardened Gemini 2.5 still 40.8%–94.6% ASR under adaptive attack** (email/TAP 99.8 -> 53.6; calendar/TAP 100 -> 94.6). The public blog for the same work contains no numbers and calls it "our most secure model family."
- **Anthropic system cards** ([Opus 4.5](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/64823ba7485345a7/Claude-Opus-4-5-System-Card.pdf), [Sonnet 4.5](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/12f214efcc2f457a/original/Claude-Sonnet-4-5-System-Card.pdf)): coding IPI no safeguards, 1 vs 200 attempts: Opus 4.5 ext-thinking 0.3% / 10.0%; Sonnet 4.5 standard 29.9% / 87.5%. Computer use Sonnet 4.5 at 200 attempts: 85.7% without safeguards, **92.9% with** (the classifier made it worse). Static 500-case tool-use set: 4% ASR — vs 70–87% for the same family under Shade at k=200, a ~20x understatement. Newer per [How we contain Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude): Opus 4.7 ~0.1% single-attempt, ~5–6% after 100 adaptive attempts on Gray Swan ART.

**Rule the benchmarks force on you:** any ASR under ~5% without a stated attacker budget (k attempts, optimizer, defense visibility) is an artifact of a weak attacker. Only numbers with a k attached mean anything in 2026.

## 6. Guardrail / classifier products

**Bottom line: no input classifier survives an adaptive attacker; the honest ceiling is "probabilistic filter that raises the bar and provides telemetry."** Market signal: every independent injection startup was absorbed in 2025 (Lakera -> Check Point, ~$300M, [PR](https://www.checkpoint.com/press-releases/check-point-acquires-lakera-to-deliver-end-to-end-ai-security-for-enterprises/); Invariant -> Snyk, Jun 24 2025, [Snyk](https://snyk.io/news/snyk-acquires-invariant-labs-to-accelerate-agentic-ai-security-innovation/); Protect AI -> Palo Alto, closed Jul 22 2025, [PANW](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/company/press/2025/palo-alto-networks-completes-acquisition-of-protect-ai); Robust Intelligence -> Cisco AI Defense).

**The decisive independent paper:** [Bypassing LLM Guardrails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11168) (Hackett et al., ACL LLMSEC 2025): Azure Prompt Shield, Protect AI v1/v2, Meta Prompt Guard, NeMo Guard Jailbreak Detect, Vijil. Baseline injection detection already uneven (Prompt Guard 99.85%, Azure 58.9%, Protect AI v1 38.3%). **Emoji smuggling: 100% evasion against essentially every system.** Zero-width chars and homoglyphs: 100% against several. Corroborated by Cisco's 2024 Prompt-Guard-86M bypass (spacing -> 0.2% detection, [Cisco](https://blogs.cisco.com/security/bypassing-metas-llama-classifier-a-simple-jailbreak), older, fixed in v2 but class persists), Mindgard vs Azure (78–100% reduction, 2024, older), and the 2026 out-of-band adaptive evaluation ([arXiv:2606.26479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26479)): detection-based guardrails give "no guarantee against adaptive attackers."

**False positives are equally disqualifying** ([InjecGuard/NotInject](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.22770)): over-defense accuracy on benign sentences containing trigger words — Meta PromptGuard 0.88%, Protect AI v2 56.6%, Lakera Guard 87.6%. 2026 tutor study ([arXiv:2605.06669](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06669)): NeMo Guardrails 0% bypass but 16.2% FPR and 1,470 ms; Prompt Guard 38.5% bypass, 3.6% FPR. AWS Bedrock's PROMPT_ATTACK filter fires on ordinary imperative documentation ([issue](https://github.com/aws-samples/sample-autonomous-cloud-coding-agents/issues/56)).

## Synthesis for a cross-company peer-agent channel

1. **Nothing labels the peer's content as untrusted for you.** MCP and A2A carry identity and transport security only; the spec text on injection is SHOULD-level. You must label it yourself (MCP Colors-style `_meta`, or your own envelope) and, per the role-confusion result, also *destyle* it (re-render into your own neutral format, strip tags/markdown/pseudo-role markers) before it reaches any privileged model. Classification: emerging convention, you build it.
2. **Apply Rule of Two per session.** If the peer channel is red, the session holding it must not also hold blue (state change / external comms / private data). Structural pattern that fits: LLM map-reduce — a quarantined worker with no tools reads the peer message and emits a fixed schema; the orchestrator never sees raw peer bytes. Context-minimize afterwards. This is the shape Claude Code's auto-mode classifier already uses (judge never sees tool results).
3. **Close the two closeable things:** egress (allow-list *and* verify the allow-list can't rot — ForcedLeak, AgentFlayer) and config/code-write surfaces (Cursor x5, Copilot, Claude Code hooks). Every incident above that got a durable fix was a capability removal (CamoLeak images, Semantic Kernel `eval`/`[KernelFunction]`), never a smarter filter.
4. **Do not rely on human confirmation** (13.6% catch rate) or on a classifier alone (100% emoji evasion; >90% adaptive ASR). Use a classifier for telemetry; use a sandbox with no real credentials and restricted network for safety (Anthropic, Rehberger, Willison all converge here).
5. **Assume worm dynamics.** ClawWorm propagated over peer agent messaging at 64.5%; the Word worm copies itself into generated output. Anything your agent *emits* back to the peer or to third parties should itself be treated as possibly carrying the payload — apply the same destyle/schema step on the way out (Claude Code now classifier-reviews outbound SendMessage; do the equivalent).
6. **Budget with k, not single-attempt ASR.** Frontier models are ~0.1–0.5% per attempt and 5–10% at 100–200 attempts; a peer that talks to you thousands of times a day is a k=1000 attacker by construction.
